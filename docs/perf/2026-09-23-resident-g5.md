## Result

| gate | measured | pass | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| S2, slot kernel energy statistics | mean shift 0.008 sd, standard deviation shift 0.003 sd | within 0.05 sd | passed |
| S4, probe rate through the pool, one gate | ratio 1.036 median (0.980 to 1.054) | at least 0.95 | passed |

The resident runner is host-bound. One thread admits, harvests, and steps
both pools, and that thread is busy all the time. More than half of its time
goes to `default_ising_beta_range`, which the controller computes twice for
every job. The GPU is busy between one third and one half of the time.

The whole-system run found 2 nonces at or below the chain target of
−14,625,068 milli in 10 minutes. The relay-based G5 run of the same day found
none in 30 minutes.

### Rate while the controller settles

The whole-system run started with a new controller. Every 10-second window
from 10 to 600 seconds lies between 1,037 and 1,068 nonces per second. The
rate does not rise while the cutoffs settle, so the controller is not the
limit. The settling time is not measurable at this rate.

The second stream ran on the same sampler and started from the saved
controller state. Its first 40 seconds ran at 1,042 to 1,067 nonces per
second, the same as the first stream. The last two windows fell to 971 and
855. This run does not explain that drop.

## Profile of the runner

`sample` ran for 10 seconds on a `one-gate-32` run of 40 seconds (1,277
nonces per second, run seed 20260924). The thread that runs `resident::run`
was busy for all 7,701 samples.

| share of the runner thread | function |
|---:|---|
| 56 percent | `Controller::admit`. `default_ising_beta_range` takes 95 percent of that share, called once by `build_beta_schedule` and once by `segment_schedule` for each job |
| 15 percent | `SlotPool::admit`, of which `fill_h_j_matching` is 60 percent |
| 13 percent | `validate_batch` for each job |
| 4 percent | `SlotPool::reads_many`, the read decode |
| 2 percent | memory copies of the graph |
| under 1 percent | `SlotPool::commit_step` |

The S4 test builds one schedule before its timed loop and admits every job
with it, without the controller or the per-job validation. Its `run_stream`
side builds the schedule inside `encode_batch`. So S4 leaves out the
two largest costs of the runner, and it favours the pool.

The G5 CSV files give 250 microseconds of device time per 32-sweep job,
against 156 microseconds in the S4 test. Until those figures agree, this
report gives no GPU ceiling for G5.
