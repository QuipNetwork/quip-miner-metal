# Cascade on the device, gate G5, 2026-09-23

Gate G5 ran the cascade for 30 minutes on fresh nonces at the full budget of
64 reads and 14,336 sweeps. It examined 1,209 nonces per second, 44 times the
27.5 per second of the unscreened miner at that budget, and it missed its rate
gate of 4,615. The device energies were exact for all 2,175,571 jobs.

The GPU was busy about a quarter of the time. The rate is set by how the
stream schedules long batches, not by GPU work. The last section describes the
cause and the options.

## Setup

The run used `QUIP_SCREEN_CASCADE=1` in `tests/probe_screen.rs`. Six threads
drew instances from the run seed 20260923 and sent them through the public
multi-spin sampler with `cascade = true`. The code was commit `beef590`,
before the final-review fixes and the second calibration. It ran the
first-round defaults: stages 32, 128, and 256 sweeps and an overall keep of 1
in 3,000. The load average was 2.9 at the start and 3.2 at the end.

## Gate results

| check | measured | gate | result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| nonces per second, stage 0 | 1,209 | at least 4,615, 90 percent of 5,128 | missed |
| device-energy audit | 0 errors in 2,175,571 jobs | zero mismatches | passed |
| audit lane | 1 loosen at stage 1, restored after 76 s | no loosen events | missed |
| host CPU per job | 0.92 ms, instance draws included | under 0.3 ms plus the graph clone | missed |
| nonces at or below the chain target | 0 | not gated | expected at this budget |

The audit-lane loosen came at 64 misses against a bound of 63.9. The run used
the first-round miss rates, which a shared-seed defect in the calibration
harness made optimistic. The calibration report's section on independent
stage seeds gives the corrected values.

No nonce reached the chain target of −14,625,068 milli. The deepest result
was −14,620,000, 5,068 milli short. At the same budget, the deepest of the
50,000 unscreened nonces of the 2026-09-18 corpus reached −14,584,000. The
target-reaching nonces in the calibration came from a 256-read,
65,536-sweep solve.

## Cause of the rate miss

Three 60-second runs with different stage lists isolate the cause:

| stages | nonces per second | batches | commonest batch sizes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 32, 128, 256 | 1,715 | 12,556 | 3,205 of 1 job, 2,586 of 20, 1,739 of 2 |
| 32, 256 | 1,415 | 6,922 | 3,356 of 20, 1,828 of 1 |
| 32 | 441 | 1,493 | 1,312 of 20, 151 of 1 |

The run with one probe stage is the slowest, although it forms almost no small
probe batches. Its debug log shows why. A kept nonce at the full budget forms
a batch of 1 job, because a batch holds one sweep budget and kept nonces
dispatch as they arrive. One job is 2 threadgroups on a GPU with 40 cores.
It runs for 434 ms in 6 chunks of about 87 ms. While a full-budget chunk runs,
a 20-job probe batch that otherwise takes 1 ms takes 83 ms and completes at
the chunk boundary. The command queue runs the two in order.

The per-job device times of the 30-minute run agree. The median is 1.5 ms,
the 99th percentile is 84 ms, and the 99.9th percentile is 441 ms.

The calibration cost model, 124 µs plus 2.28 µs per sweep, was fitted on
20-job batches. It is accurate for full batches and does not describe a
1-job batch, which costs 434 ms of GPU time at 2 of 40 cores. The load
controller reads the cost model, so it sees idle GPU time and loosens the
keep. A looser keep sends more nonces to the full budget and adds more of
these batches.

The spec's action for a missed rate gate is a third in-flight slot. That does
not address this cause, because the in-flight slots are not what serialises
the work.

## Options

Each option needs a decision, because two of them change a spec decision.

1. **Run full-budget chunks beside probe batches.** Give low-occupancy
   batches short chunks and a second command queue, so probe batches fill the
   GPU while a lone full-budget job runs. This keeps the spec's rule that kept
   nonces dispatch as they arrive. Whether Apple GPUs overlap work from two
   queues this way is not measured yet.
2. **Batch full-budget jobs.** Hold kept nonces for the full budget for a
   short window and dispatch them together, so one batch fills the GPU. This
   changes the spec's rule. The spec ruled out buffering because a full batch
   of 20 costs 2,560 probes of lead time. A short window, for example 50 ms,
   costs less lead time than one 434 ms lone job.
3. **Size the full budget for the cascade.** A full-budget stage with more
   reads per job fills more of the GPU per batch. That changes what a result
   contains and needs the coordinator's agreement.
